LanceFlow.

Time tracking for people who invoice by the hour. Built because I needed it — kept because freelancers did too.

RoleDesign, build, deploy — solo
StackFlask · SQLite · Vercel
StatusLive in production
TimelineBuilt in 10 days

The problem

Existing time trackers charge monthly for features freelancers don't use, and hide the one thing they do — turning tracked hours into a clean, sendable invoice — behind the paid tier. Meanwhile the hours still need tracking, the clients still need naming, and month-end still needs a PDF.

LanceFlow's scope wrote itself: clients, projects, a timer, an invoice. Nothing else. The hard part wasn't the feature list — it was making the timer trustworthy. A tracker you can't trust is a tracker you stop opening.

The build

A Flask app over SQLite, deployed to Vercel. Sessions carry clients and projects; every tracked minute is a row. Invoices assemble from tracked time with one click — rendered to PDF on the server, no browser print-dialog gymnastics.

  • The refresh-proof stopwatch — the timer stores a start timestamp server-side and recomputes elapsed time on every load. Close the tab, kill the browser, come back tomorrow: the count is still right.
  • One-click PDF invoices — grouped by client and date range, formatted like a document a client expects, not a screenshot of a dashboard.
  • SQLite, on purpose — one file, zero ops, backups are a copy. For a single-tenant tool, boring storage is the right storage.

What went wrong first

The first timer ran client-side — setInterval in the tab. Users close tabs. Every refresh meant a dead timer, lost minutes, and a tool that couldn't be trusted with its one job. Moving the source of truth server-side fixed it forever: the tab is now just a display, and displays can die without consequence.

What I learned

Building for yourself is the fastest requirements-gathering there is — I was the annoyed freelancer, so I knew exactly which three screens mattered. But the bigger lesson: boring is a feature. The stopwatch being dull — unkillable, unremarkable, always right — is why the tool gets used. I now chase that quality in everything I ship.

If you have an internal tool or a workflow living in spreadsheets, this is the kind of small, sharp app I love building.
