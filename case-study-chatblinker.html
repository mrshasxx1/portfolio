ChatBlinker.

A chat platform with no signup wall. Pick a name, join a room — or let it pair you with a stranger in seconds.

RoleDesign, build, deploy — solo
StackNode.js · WebSockets · Render
StatusLive in production
TimelineBuilt in 2 weeks

The problem

Every chat app today wants your email, your phone number, your contacts list — before you've said a single word. Sometimes you just want to talk: ask strangers about a game you're stuck in, vent about your day, or hang out in a room about a topic you care about. The signup wall kills exactly that.

So the constraint was set before any code: zero signup. A name and a click. The product question was how to make an identity-free chat still feel safe and worth returning to.

The build

ChatBlinker is a Node.js server speaking WebSockets, with three modes that share one message layer: public rooms organized by topic, a random 1-on-1 matcher that pairs waiting strangers the moment one appears, and private DMs for conversations worth keeping.

  • WebSocket-first — every message is a push, not a poll. Chats feel instant because they are.
  • Stateless identities — no accounts to leak, no passwords to hash, no database of people. It ships nothing worth stealing.
  • The matcher — the random 1-on-1 queue was the fun part: pair two waiting sockets, handle the disconnect mid-conversation, requeue cleanly.

What went wrong first

The first version let refresh kills linger — a closed tab left a "ghost" sitting in the random queue, and matches took longer and longer as ghosts piled up. The fix was heartbeat pings: if a socket stops answering, it's gone, free the partner, requeue. Ten lines, and the matcher went from flaky to boring.

What I learned

Real-time is a different discipline from request-response. You're managing connections, not pages — and every edge case is about people leaving: refreshes, dead batteries, subway tunnels. Designing for graceful exits taught me more than building the happy path did.

If you need real-time features in your product — live dashboards, collaborative tools, chat — this project is the proof I can ship them end-to-end.
