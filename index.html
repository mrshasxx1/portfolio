Depends on scope, not on a rate card. A focused web app usually starts around a few hundred dollars; I'll give you a fixed quote after one conversation — no hourly surprises.

How long does it take?+

Small tools: about a week. Full apps with accounts and payments: two to six weeks. You get a timeline with the quote, and I stick to it.

One short call to understand the problem → a written scope and fixed quote → I build, you see progress every few days → we ship, deploy, and you own everything: code, domains, accounts.

Yes — a free bug-fix window with every project, then optional monthly care if you want someone watching it. No lock-in either way.

Dev log.

Short notes from actually building things — what worked, what didn't, what I'd do differently.

StreamForge2026 · 4 min

The upload that ate the server

My first StreamForge buffered the whole video in memory before processing — a single big upload could take down the free-tier server. The fix, streaming chunks to disk, is ten lines. Finding it took two days and one very long Render log.

Read the case study→
LanceFlow2025 · 3 min

A stopwatch that survives refresh

Freelancers close tabs. LanceFlow's first timer died with them. Storing the start timestamp server-side and recomputing elapsed time on load made the timer boring — which is exactly what a timer should be. Boring is a feature.

Open LanceFlow→
MegaCalc2025 · 3 min

Why eval() is never the answer

The lazy way to build a calculator is eval(userInput). The right way is a tokenizer and a small parser. I built the lazy version first, broke it on purpose, then built the parser — and finally understood why Python ships an ast module.

Open MegaCalc→
